Privacy-safe neighborhoods for Denmark

With Two Sigma Data Clinic and Aalborg University, I helped build open-source geographic units for Denmark: regions that follow real roads and waterways while meeting strict privacy thresholds. We presented the work at SDSC 2022.

Partners
Two Sigma Data Clinic · Aalborg University (BUILD)
Output
Open-source code on GitHub
Methods
OpenStreetMap polygonization, max-p regionalization

The problem

Danish municipalities are too large and uneven to guide local action. Detailed administrative data exists on a 100 m grid, but individual grid cells break privacy rules: every published unit must contain at least 150 people and 75 households.

Clustering grid cells directly did not work. Empty cells were assigned at random, and suburban and rural regions came out oddly shaped and hard to use for analysis.

Approach

  1. Polygons from the built environment. Each municipality is split step by step along the OpenStreetMap road network: motorways and primary roads first, then waterways, secondary and tertiary roads, then residential streets.
  2. Map the grid onto the polygons. We compared a spatial join with interpolation (smooth boundaries, but assumes population is spread evenly within a cell) against a grid overlay (exact counts, less natural edges).
  3. Regionalize. Max-p regionalization with rook contiguity merges polygons until each region meets the 150-person and 75-household threshold.
  4. Evaluate. Checked that the privacy rule held across the past ten years so the units support time-series work, reviewed the distribution of region compactness, and inspected maps visually.

Outcome
